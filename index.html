Why these parts

  • Processor: 12 of AMD's newer Zen 5 cores. Sweeps run 1 case per core, and 32 GB of RAM feeds about 10 cases at once, so 12 cores match the RAM. Each run is as fast as on the 16-core chip. It is new, runs cooler at 120 W, and costs about £80 less than the used 16-core 9950X.
  • RAM: a factory-matched pair of 2 x 16 GB. 2 sticks run at full speed on this platform, and 4 sticks would run slower. A second matching pair later gives 64 GB.
  • Motherboard: good build quality and power delivery, with room for a 16-core chip later. Its 2 USB-C ports carry video, so it runs 3 screens with no graphics card. It also takes newer AM5 chips, expected in 2027.
  • Cooler: a large twin-tower air cooler with two 140 mm fans. Big fans turn slowly, so it stays quiet on long sweeps. It has no pump to fail, it is easier to fit than a liquid cooler, and it can handle a 16-core chip later.
  • Power supply: 850 W Gold with a 10-year warranty. The PC peaks at about 240 W, which leaves room for a gaming card later.
  • Case and fans: a high-airflow case with a mesh front and a glass side. It comes with 4 fans: 3 RGB fans at the front blowing in, and 1 at the back blowing out. A hub runs all the fans and lights from one header. It costs £30 less than the curved-glass case and suits an air cooler better.
  • No graphics card yet: the built-in graphics use about 1 to 2 GB of RAM. If RAM fills up during sweeps, a used card can be added in minutes.

Build and setup notes

Fan layout: leave the case fans where they come fitted. The 3 RGB front fans blow air in, and the rear fan blows it out. Fit the cooler so its fans blow towards the back of the case. The case fans plug into the case hub, and the cooler plugs into CPU_FAN.

Build order

  1. Fit the processor, RAM and SSD on the motherboard while it sits on its box, then put the board in the case. Fit the cooler after that. Peel the plastic film off its base before fitting it. Keep the socket cover on until the processor goes in, and drop the chip in without sliding it.
  2. Put the RAM in slots A2 and B2, the second and fourth slots from the processor.
  3. Plug in the processor power cables at the top of the board before fitting the cooler. They are hard to reach afterwards.
  4. Put the SSD in the top M.2 slot.
  5. On first start, wait at least 5 minutes. The board tests the RAM and can show a black screen meanwhile.

BIOS settings

  • Turn on EXPO for the RAM. Until then it runs at a slower default speed, usually 4800 or 5600. EXPO is a factory-tested overclock profile that sets 6400 CL32 at 1.35 V in one click. If it is not stable at 6400, set it to 6000, which is just as good in practice.
  • Set UMA Frame Buffer Size to 512 MB, so the screens use as little RAM as possible.
  • Set a quiet fan curve.

Day one checks: run MemTest86 overnight, and check the SSD's health with CrystalDiskInfo. Do this while the CeX returns window is still open.

ScanProtect: the Scan order has ScanProtect, which covers damage during installation, including bent motherboard pins. It runs for 28 days from delivery, and damage must be reported within 24 hours of finding it. Build within that window.
